# Evolutionary Bioinformatics of *Odocoileus virginianus*: CytB, Molecular Entropy, and the Structure of Genetic Diversity

## 1. Introduction

The white-tailed deer (*Odocoileus virginianus*) is a widely distributed cervid species in the Americas, exhibiting substantial geographic, ecological, and morphological variation. Understanding its genetic diversity and evolutionary history requires integrating molecular sequence analysis, computational methods, and quantitative approaches to genetic variability. Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA), particularly the cytochrome b gene (*MT-CYB*), provides a valuable molecular marker for investigating nucleotide variation, haplotype diversity, and evolutionary relationships among populations. This research develops a reproducible evolutionary bioinformatics framework for examining mitochondrial CytB sequence variation in *O. virginianus*. The analytical approach combines sequence retrieval and curation, multiple sequence alignment, identification of polymorphic nucleotide sites, codon-position analysis, Shannon entropy, and Walsh–Hadamard-based mathematical transformations. Shannon entropy is employed to quantify nucleotide uncertainty and positional variability across aligned sequences, while Walsh–Hadamard transformations are explored as complementary mathematical tools for characterizing patterns in the genetic data. By integrating conventional molecular analyses with information-theoretic and spectral approaches, this study aims to characterize the structure and distribution of mitochondrial genetic diversity in *O. virginianus*.

## 2. Research Objective

To characterize mitochondrial CytB genetic variability in *Odocoileus virginianus* through evolutionary bioinformatics, Shannon entropy, and Walsh–Hadamard-based mathematical analyses.

## 3. Specific Objectives

1. Retrieve, curate, and validate mitochondrial CytB sequences from public nucleotide databases.
2. Identify nucleotide polymorphisms, variable sites, and codon-specific patterns across aligned sequences.
3. Quantify positional genetic variability using Shannon entropy.
4. Explore genetic variation patterns using Walsh–Hadamard mathematical transformations.
5. Integrate molecular and computational results to investigate the structure of mitochondrial genetic diversity.

## 4. Methodological Framework

The research workflow comprises:

- **Sequence retrieval:** NCBI GenBank and Entrez Direct.
- **Sequence curation:** Taxonomic verification, gene annotation, sequence quality assessment, and filtering.
- **Multiple sequence alignment:** MAFFT.
- **Genetic variability:** Variable nucleotide sites, substitutions, and codon-position analysis.
- **Information theory:** Positional Shannon entropy.
- **Mathematical transformations:** Walsh–Hadamard analysis.
- **Evolutionary analysis:** Phylogenetic inference and comparative sequence analysis.
- **Reproducibility:** Python, Biopython, SeqKit, shell scripts, and documented computational environments.

## 5. Scientific Reproducibility

Scripts and supporting materials will be incorporated progressively, following the methodological structure of the study. Data provenance, analytical parameters, and software versions will be documented to facilitate independent verification and reproducibility.
